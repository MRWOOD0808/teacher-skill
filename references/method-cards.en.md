# Method Cards — English Backup

These cards are modern transfer designs derived from the studied text of *Selected Works of Mao Zedong*. They are not quotations from the author and do not imply that all historical conclusions remain valid today.

## 1. Minimum Investigation Card

| Item | Question to answer |
|---|---|
| Decision | What decision will this investigation support? |
| Object | Which people, processes, events, or data directly affect it? |
| History | When did the problem appear, and which events changed the trend? |
| Sampling | Which typical, extreme, intermediate, and counterexample cases must be covered? |
| Facts | What can be directly observed or verified from primary records? |
| Interpretations | How do different stakeholders explain the same fact? |
| Falsification | What finding would overturn the current hypothesis? |
| Saturation | When does new evidence stop changing the main judgment? |

An investigation meeting or interview needs a central question and outline while allowing participants to supplement and challenge one another. The facilitator should listen and take notes directly. For sensitive issues, prefer separate interviews or anonymous channels; never use a group meeting to suppress minority views.

## 2. Evidence Ledger

| ID | Claim | Type | Source/time | Supporting evidence | Counterevidence | Reliability | To verify |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E1 |  | fact/interpretation/hypothesis/value |  |  |  | high/medium/low |  |

Primary records and reproducible observations are usually stronger than retellings, but primary data still require checks for definitions, missingness, and selection bias. Every conclusion must trace back to the ledger; an essay title or author’s authority cannot substitute for facts.

## 3. Contradiction–Stage Card

| Relationship | Two parties/requirements | Interdependence | Conflict symptoms | Current dominant factor | Trend | Evidence | Intervention point |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Rank relationships using four considerations: constraint on the objective, tractability, time urgency, and leverage over other problems. Scores support discussion; they do not mechanically determine the answer.

Bind stage changes to observable signals such as demand shifts, access to a critical resource, regulatory change, movement in a key group’s position, or a pilot crossing a threshold. Re-rank after the stage changes.

## 4. Stakeholder Card

| Stakeholder | Legitimate interest | Observable behavior | Influence | Support conditions | Concerns/resistance | Engagement | Rights protection |
|---|---|---|---:|---|---|---|---|

Do not treat group identity as evidence of position. Even for explicit obstruction, describe observable behavior, risk, and lawful response only. Do not use humiliation, deception, harassment, surveillance, or coercion.

## 5. Focus and Support Card

- **Focused investment:** work that directly breaks the current bottleneck.
- **Necessary support:** safeguards without which the central task will fail.
- **Deferred list:** valuable work that would disperse resources at this stage.

State the cost of each category. If the claimed priority does not stop other work, transfer resources, or change sequence, it is probably not a real priority.

## 6. General–Particular Experiment Card

1. Form an initial common hypothesis from several cases; do not turn one case into a law.
2. Pilot in representative, boundary, and counterexample settings.
3. Predefine metrics, failure signals, stop conditions, and ethical boundaries.
4. Record implementation deviations and external changes.
5. Revise the general proposal from results before extending it to new cases.

Pilots must be voluntary, lawful, and as reversible as possible. Do not use high-risk populations or irreversible systems for unreviewed experiments.

## 7. Execution and Review Card

| Action | Owner | Authority | Deadline | Inputs/resources | Leading indicator | Outcome metric | Failure/exit signal |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Use collective discussion and a clear decision for major issues, then individual accountability in execution. In the review, compare **prediction → actual result → cause of deviation → model update → next action**. Avoid reviews that only celebrate results or only blame an individual.
